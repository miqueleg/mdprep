# MCPB Hessian-method benchmark

The current benchmark is the curated 7E07 Fe(III), α-ketoglutarate, and
proline site under `examples/tutorials/7E07_bonded_fe3/`. It contains an
open-shell transition metal, two histidine donors, one aspartate donor, and a
bidentate coordinating cofactor. This is a more demanding validation target
than an isolated closed-shell zinc ion.

The completed comparisons include g-xTB, GFN2-xTB, MACE-POLAR-1, and a
conventional MCPB DFT reference. The external ORCA 6.1 result was returned,
checksum-validated, converted to the MCPB formatted-checkpoint interface, and
processed by the same MCPB.py Seminario implementation.

## Controlled-comparison requirements

Candidate and reference must use the same:

- MCPB small-model PDB coordinates and exact atom order/identity;
- Fe oxidation state, charge, and sextet multiplicity;
- protonation and capping model;
- five declared Fe-donor bonds;
- MCPB atom types, Seminario variant, and scale factor; and
- MCPB.py/AmberTools version where practical.

The intended independent variable is the Hessian model. Geometry, charge,
multiplicity, declared coordination, MCPB version, and Seminario settings
remain identical. Method-specific numerical controls and executables are
recorded rather than treated as shared variables. Comparing different optimized
geometries is useful separately, but it does not isolate parameter sensitivity
to the Hessian model.

## Integrated workflow

The tutorial manifest already selects `proline_substrate`,
`alpha_ketoglutarate`, and `catalytic_fe3` in the refinement QM region, with Fe
multiplicity 6 and total QM multiplicity 6. The MCPB bonds supply the complete
coordinating protein residues. Under `mcpb.pyscf`, it uses:

```yaml
geometry_source: qmmm_refinement
hessian_backend: xtb
xtb:
  model: gfn2
  executable: xtb
  accuracy: 0.001
  num_threads: 1
```

Keep the PySCF method/basis/resource settings: they supply the embedded
large-model ESP used by MCPB RESP, not the candidate Hessian. Run the complete
preparation and preserve the generated `catalytic_fe3_small.pdb`, xTB Hessian
report, MCPB `frcmod`, final topology, and all validation reports.

The equivalent general schema is validated in
`examples/11_qmmm_metal_gfn2_mcpb.yaml`.

## Conventional MCPB DFT reference

The controlled reference uses the established MCPB small-model force-constant
protocol: unrestricted B3LYP/6-31G*, charge 0, multiplicity 6, on the exact
relaxed 55-atom geometry. In ORCA, `B3LYP/G` is required to select the
Gaussian-compatible B3LYP definition and `6-31G(d)` is the equivalent basis
notation. `NoRI` avoids introducing an RI/COSX difference. `VeryTightSCF`,
`DEFGRID3`, and an explicit UKS stability analysis provide additional
numerical and electronic-state checks.

The portable job and returned result are under:

```text
prepared/benchmarks/7E07_relaxed_xtb_compare/orca_reference_bundle/
prepared/benchmarks/7E07_relaxed_xtb_compare/orca_reference_return/run_001/
```

It pins the XYZ and PDB with SHA-256 checksums, requests 16 ORCA processes,
preserves the raw unprojected Cartesian Hessian, validates normal termination,
and creates a checksummed return archive. See its `README.md` for the portable
execution and validation procedure. The local conversion/comparison is
reproducible with
`prepared/benchmarks/7E07_relaxed_xtb_compare/run_orca_reference_compare.py`;
this benchmark adapter is intentionally not an mdprep ORCA backend.

After return, MCPB.py must process the ORCA-derived Cartesian Hessian with the
same five bonds, atom types, Seminario implementation, scale factor, and
relaxed coordinates as the g-xTB branch. The primary comparison is every
metal-involving bond force constant; metal-centered and donor-adjacent angle
terms remain part of the report because they are generated by the same MCPB
step and can expose failures not visible in the bonds alone.

For the integrated PySCF alternative, copy the candidate's exact generated
`catalytic_fe3_small.pdb` to a stable reference path and use:

```yaml
pyscf:
  geometry_source: external_pdb
  optimized_small_model_pdb: reference/catalytic_fe3_small.qmmm_refined.pdb
  small_model_geometry_status: user_optimized
  hessian_backend: pyscf
  method: B3LYP
  basis: "6-31G*"
```

The tutorial's B3LYP/6-31G* setting matches the same established MCPB setup.
Record SCF convergence, stability, spin contamination, Hessian symmetry,
imaginary modes, method/basis, software version, resource use, and runtime. A
DFT calculation that did not converge to the reviewed electronic state is not
an acceptable reference.

The reference may also be produced through MCPB's external Gaussian/GAMESS
route. In either case, MCPB.py must process the DFT Hessian with the same
Seminario settings used for the candidate.

## Returned conventional-reference comparison

The ORCA 6.1.1 calculation completed on 2026-08-13 after a 15 h 15 min Hessian
run using 16 processes. Both jobs terminated normally, stderr was empty, and
the returned archive and every internal result checksum passed. The UKS
`B3LYP/G 6-31G(d)` wavefunction was stable; its final Hessian energy was
-2710.168288803181 Eh and `<S^2>` was 8.761477 versus 8.75 for an ideal sextet.
The 165 by 165 Cartesian Hessian was finite and exactly symmetric at its
printed precision.

The controlled geometry, charge 0, multiplicity 6, atom order, five declared
Fe bonds, MCPB types, and scale factor were identical in all three branches.
All 22 generated terms matched by identity and their equilibrium distances and
angles were identical. The force-constant comparison was:

| Candidate versus ORCA/MCPB | Bond RMSE (kcal mol^-1 Å^-2) | Angle RMSE (kcal mol^-1 rad^-2) |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| MACE-POLAR-1-M | 2.22 | 18.35 |
| g-xTB | 6.10 | 23.63 |
| GFN2-xTB | 16.22 | 25.04 |

For bonds, g-xTB is substantially closer to this conventional calculation
than GFN2-xTB at the controlled geometry. For angles, g-xTB has a slightly
lower absolute RMSE. Relative errors require caution because the DFT reference
contains zero or very small values: only two of five reference bond constants
are positive, and the 2.01 kcal mol^-1 rad^-2 `Y1-M1-Y2` reference angle makes
the g-xTB relative angle RMSE especially large.

The conventional result is not a valid production reference at this fixed
geometry. ORCA found 16 significant imaginary modes, with the lowest at
-3080.12 cm^-1. That mode is localized predominantly on the distal
α-ketoglutarate carboxylate (`C5`, `O3`, and `O4`), not on Fe. MCPB obtained
negative raw constants for both Fe–AKG bonds (-18.8 and -16.9), Fe–Asp OD1
(-2.6), and the `Y2-M1-Y5` angle (-15.62), then applied its documented clamp
to zero. Consequently both xTB-versus-DFT reports correctly have status
`FAIL`.

The useful conclusion is limited but clear: g-xTB resembles the conventional
MCPB bond constants more closely than GFN2-xTB on this geometry, including an
excellent Fe–HID116 result (21.1 versus 21.5). It does not establish that
g-xTB is quantitatively correct. A decisive reference requires a DFT-relaxed
small cluster or a consistent QM/MM/partial-Hessian treatment that removes the
fixed-cluster instabilities before Seminario parameters are judged. Detailed
JSON, CSV, Markdown, converted Hessian, MCPB log, and `frcmod` files are under
`prepared/benchmarks/7E07_relaxed_xtb_compare/orca_reference_comparison/`.

## MACE-POLAR-1 comparison

The MACE-POLAR-1-M calculation used the same 55 atoms, byte-identical XYZ,
charge 0, multiplicity 6, and MCPB inputs as all three calculations above. It
used MACE 0.3.16, PyTorch 2.13.0, ASE 3.29.0, graph-longrange 0.4.0, float64
inference, and four CPU threads. The official checkpoint SHA-256 was
`fab8b8713c832f31a2a853aaa22fd638be8a369cbf5095e6b3e982a18d10e93a`.

The analytical 165 by 165 Hessian required 544.6 seconds inside the worker
(547.2 seconds including process overhead). Its maximum antisymmetry was
5.21e-9 Eh/bohr². A deterministic central finite-difference check of one
Hessian-vector product had relative error 1.49e-5, independently confirming
the force sign, atom ordering, and unit conversion used by the adapter.

The MACE energy was -2710.326511400320 Eh and the gradient norm was 0.1209
Eh/bohr. The raw Cartesian Hessian had 11 eigenvalues below -1e-8 Eh/bohr²,
again showing that this extracted capped geometry is not stationary for the
candidate method. MCPB projected three negative Fe-donor bond constants and
clamped them to zero:

| Bond | Raw MACE | Final MACE | ORCA | g-xTB | GFN2-xTB |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Fe-AKG O1 (`M1-Y1`) | -15.7 | 0.0 | 0.0 | 10.4 | 11.5 |
| Fe-AKG O2 (`M1-Y2`) | -15.0 | 0.0 | 0.0 | 2.2 | 5.2 |
| Fe-His N (`M1-Y3`) | 16.6 | 16.6 | 21.5 | 21.1 | 29.8 |
| Fe-Asp O (`M1-Y4`) | -6.7 | 0.0 | 0.0 | 0.7 | 27.5 |
| Fe-His N (`M1-Y5`) | 11.0 | 11.0 | 11.8 | 20.3 | 30.0 |

All 22 terms and equilibrium values matched by identity. The complete
method-to-method comparison is:

| Comparison | Bond RMSE | Bond relative RMSE | Angle RMSE | Angle relative RMSE |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| MACE versus ORCA | 2.2204 | 0.1681 | 18.3483 | 3.2540 |
| MACE versus g-xTB | 6.6367 | 0.8069 | 18.3800 | 0.4198 |
| MACE versus GFN2-xTB | 17.0340 | 0.8482 | 27.7273 | 6.5044 |

The small MACE-versus-ORCA bond RMSE is not evidence that the MACE parameters
are production-ready: the statistic is dominated by the same three bonds being
negative and then set to zero in both methods. For the two positive ORCA bond
terms, MACE is reasonably close (16.6 versus 21.5 and 11.0 versus 11.8), and its
absolute angle RMSE is the lowest of the three candidates. Nevertheless, three
zero bonded parameters make this MACE parameter set invalid. The controlled
result supports MACE-POLAR-1 as a fast additional diagnostic, not as an
automatic replacement for a stationary, chemically reviewed reference.

The reproducible driver, complete provenance JSON, raw Hessian, worker report,
MCPB logs, `frcmod`, and per-method CSV/JSON/Markdown comparisons are under
`prepared/benchmarks/7E07_relaxed_xtb_compare/mace_polar1_comparison/`.

## Method-specific relaxation and Hessian comparison

A second 2026-08-13 comparison evaluates g-xTB and MACE-POLAR-1 on capped
55-atom MCPB clusters generated from their respective converged
mechanical-embedding QM/MM minima. Both branches use charge 0, multiplicity 6,
the same five explicitly declared Fe bonds, the same ligand parameters, and
the same MCPB.py Seminario implementation. Unlike the controlled comparison
above, both the relaxation method and Hessian method vary, so the result
measures the combined method-specific workflow rather than Hessian sensitivity
at one geometry.

The finite Fe-donor force constants are moderately different overall: bond
force-constant RMSE is 8.44 kcal mol^-1 Å^-2 and relative RMSE is 0.223. The
largest relative finite-bond difference is Fe--AKG O1, 32.2 from g-xTB versus
20.3 kcal mol^-1 Å^-2 from MACE (37%). The protein donor bonds are closer:
67.8 versus 55.5 for His114, 43.7 versus 50.7 for Asp116, and 57.2 versus 53.4
for His214. Angle force-constant RMSE is 10.07 kcal mol^-1 rad^-2 and relative
RMSE is 0.231.

Neither output is a valid production bonded model. During both full-system
relaxations, water HOH610 approached Fe to about 1.93 Å and replaced the
declared AKG O5 interaction; Fe--AKG O5 lengthened to 2.592 Å with g-xTB and
2.526 Å with MACE. MCPB consequently generated a zero Fe--AKG O5 force
constant in both branches, and the comparison status is `FAIL`. mdprep does
not silently add the new Fe--water bond. For this diagnostic only, solvent was
explicitly omitted before building the retained five-bond MCPB cluster.

The capped clusters also remain nonstationary even though their parent QM/MM
optimizations converged. Their gradient norms are 0.0775 and 0.0801 Eh/bohr,
with six and seven negative Cartesian Hessian eigenvalues for g-xTB and MACE,
respectively. MACE's analytic Hessian passed its deterministic Hessian-vector
finite-difference check with relative error 2.34e-6. Complete branch summaries,
raw Hessians, MCPB logs, `frcmod` files, and the term-by-term report are under
`prepared/benchmarks/7E07_relaxed_xtb_compare/method_specific_hessian_mcpb/`.

## Fixed crystallographic heavy atoms with proton-only GFN2 refinement

The 2026-08-14 follow-up preserves every 7E07 experimental heavy-atom
coordinate. The ff14SB/TIP3P provisional build added hydrogens and supplied
AM1-BCC ligand/cofactor charges. GFN2/OpenMM electrostatic-embedding QM/MM then
optimized only 355 active-region hydrogens; all 2,344 heavy atoms and 2,108
out-of-region hydrogens had exactly zero displacement relative to the Amber
restart coordinates. The normal 78-atom QM region, charge 0, sextet spin, 4 Å
non-water cutoff, and 8 Å water cutoff were retained.

The subsequent 55-atom g-xTB/MCPB Seminario calculation generated positive
force constants for all five declared Fe bonds: 30.1 (His114), 43.7 (Asp116),
59.8 (His214), 106.2 (AKG O1), and 65.3 kcal mol^-1 Å^-2 (AKG O5). HOH610
remained 4.224 Å from Fe and was not inferred as a coordinator. This avoids the
coordination change seen when heavy atoms were allowed to relax.

The capped cluster is intentionally not a g-xTB minimum: its gradient norm is
0.2055 Eh/bohr and it has eight negative Cartesian Hessian eigenvalues. The
result is a complete fixed-experimental-geometry parameter set, not
vibrational-minimum validation. Full provenance and artifacts are under
`prepared/benchmarks/7E07_relaxed_xtb_compare/crystal_fixed_heavy_gfn2_gxtb/`.

## Current g-xTB versus GFN2-xTB comparison

Run:

```bash
mdprep compare-mcpb \
  gxtb/catalytic_fe3_mcpbpy.frcmod \
  gfn2/catalytic_fe3_mcpbpy.frcmod \
  --candidate-label "g-xTB Seminario" \
  --reference-label "GFN2-xTB Seminario" \
  --output-dir comparison/7E07_gxtb_vs_gfn2 \
  --report-only
```

The first comparison should be report-only with no guessed scientific cutoff.
Review:

- every Fe-donor bond force constant and equilibrium distance;
- every Fe-centered and donor-adjacent angle force constant;
- relative RMSE, maximum relative difference, and outlier identity;
- numerical stability when xTB accuracy is tightened;
- DFT and xTB vibrational spectra and imaginary modes; and
- whether disagreement is localized or systematic.

Non-positive force constants always make a comparison status `FAIL`, even in
report-only mode. Report-only mode preserves every term and diagnostic rather
than accepting the parameter set. Only after reviewing valid distributions
should system-specific automated limits be added to
`mcpb.parameter_comparison`.

## Preserved unrelaxed 7E07 diagnostic

The controlled 2026-08-11 run used the same 55-atom geometry, charge 0,
multiplicity 6, xTB 6.7.1 executable, accuracy 0.001, and one thread. All 22
MCPB terms matched by identity, and all equilibrium values were identical
because the geometry was held fixed.

GFN2-xTB nevertheless generated one zero Fe-donor bond force constant and
seven zero angle force constants after MCPB.py clamped negative Seminario
values to zero. The comparison therefore has status `FAIL`. Among the
non-zero reference terms, bond force-constant relative RMSE was 3.003 and angle
force-constant relative RMSE was 74.293; the latter is dominated by a GFN2
angle constant of 0.08 kcal mol^-1 rad^-2. These relative statistics are not
meaningful as validation metrics when reference values approach zero.

This run used the preserved unoptimized MCPB test geometry, whose GFN2 gradient
norm was 0.2227 Eh/Bohr and which had ten significant imaginary frequencies.
It establishes that this GFN2 parameter set is unusable, but it does not prove
that g-xTB is quantitatively correct. Repeat the comparison after the complete
QM/MM relaxation before making a production force-field choice.

## Relaxed 7E07 comparison

The follow-up 2026-08-11 run used the structure after 168 converged ASH
GFN2-xTB/OpenMM optimization steps. The QM region contained 78 atoms (charge
0, multiplicity 6); 717 atoms were active. Complete protein contact residues
used the 4 Å cutoff and protonated contact waters used the 8 Å cutoff. The
preserved full relaxed structure is
`prepared/benchmarks/7E07_relaxed_xtb_compare/gfn2_primary/intermediate/02_qmmm_refined_hydrogenated.pdb`.

MCPB.py extracted the same 55-atom capped model for both Hessians. The staged
XYZ SHA-256 was
`5c83a85366d855f7c4885998438178fedf148ad1719f7c5cf8d754137d337d41` in both
branches. Charge 0, multiplicity 6, xTB 6.7.1, accuracy 0.001, one thread,
five declared Fe bonds, atom types, and Seminario settings were identical.
GFN2-xTB required 10.79 seconds and g-xTB 61.89 seconds for the Hessian.

The capped gas-phase cluster is not itself a stationary point even though the
parent QM/MM optimization converged: GFN2 and g-xTB reported gradient norms of
0.0972 and 0.1274 Eh/Bohr, with four and six significant imaginary
frequencies, respectively. This is an extraction/capping diagnostic and must
remain visible when interpreting the parameter comparison.

All 22 MCPB terms matched by identity and all equilibrium values were exactly
equal. Every generated force constant was positive, so the comparison status
is `REPORT_ONLY` rather than `FAIL`; no acceptance thresholds were invented.
The numerical disagreement is nevertheless large:

| Quantity | Result |
| --- | ---: |
| Bond force-constant relative RMSE | 0.5443 |
| Bond force-constant RMSE | 13.40 kcal mol^-1 Å^-2 |
| Angle force-constant relative RMSE | 4.1024 |
| Angle force-constant RMSE | 23.23 kcal mol^-1 rad^-2 |
| Equilibrium-value RMSE | 0 for bonds and angles |

The most concerning bond is Fe–Asp OD1: g-xTB gives 0.7 versus 27.5 kcal
mol^-1 Å^-2 from GFN2-xTB. The largest relative angle difference is
`M1-Y2-c`: 36.52 versus 2.09 kcal mol^-1 rad^-2. Therefore mdprep does not
select either model automatically or treat positivity as production
validation. The JSON/CSV/Markdown reports are preserved under
`prepared/benchmarks/7E07_relaxed_xtb_compare/parameter_comparison/`.

GFN2 relaxation also moved the undeclared ASP 116 OD2 atom to 2.374 Å from Fe.
The benchmark retains the explicitly curated five-coordinate model (ASP OD1
bonded, OD2 a close nonbonded contact); it does not silently convert Asp to a
bidentate coordinator.

The shared embedded PySCF large-model RESP attempt was stopped after its
open-shell SCF oscillated by multiple hartree. Thus the ligand AM1-BCC files
and both complete bonded `frcmod` comparisons are available, but no final
RESP-completed MD topology is claimed for this relaxed benchmark. Resolving
the electronic solution and choosing/validating a Hessian model are required
before final topology generation.

## Validation beyond parameter agreement

An `frcmod` comparison is necessary but not sufficient. The final candidate
and reference topologies should undergo the same restrained minimization and
short replicated NVT/NPT protocol. Monitor Fe-donor distance distributions,
coordination number, metal-centered angles, α-ketoglutarate chelate orientation,
energy stability, and drift as restraints are released. Compare both ensembles
with the crystallographic structure and the QM normal modes.

The bonded model remains state-specific and cannot describe redox changes,
ligand exchange, O₂ binding chemistry, or bond breaking.
